17. Looking out to the distance in Perlin zone makes "3D Render" ms shoot up to 40 ms, while looking at the ground is close to 0 ms

    **Partly addressed 2026-08-31; the rest is real and open.** This is march-step count, and it is inherent to the shape of the problem: a ray aimed at the horizon travels the whole world before it hits or exits, and for an *implicit* AST every step also pays three extra `sdfEval` calls to compute the gradient that makes the step size honest (`d / |grad f|` — see the campaign review §2 for why that correction cannot be dropped). Looking down, the first step hits.

    Taken: the march is now clamped to the **camera's far plane**, unprojected from whatever projection was actually set rather than read off a named setting. Everything past it is clipped before it is ever seen, so marching there is work with no possible output — this cannot remove anything a Person could have seen, which is exactly what distinguishes it from the hardcoded 600-unit horizon the campaign had (reverted; it made real terrain vanish). For the noise floor the far plane is 1000 while a grazing ray's AABB exit can be ~2800, so the worst rays lose over half their march. The conversion is done in field space via the unnormalised transformed direction, so it stays exact under non-uniform scale.

    **Still open, and the honest framing:** this cost was always there and was hidden behind the 20–30 ms of Ourverse metalaw sweep; with those off it is now the visible ceiling. The real answers are the ones already planned — analytic gradients emitted with the WGSL instead of three finite differences per step, and a proper Lipschitz/cone bound for heightfields (`docs/plans/FRONTIER_MULTI_HUNDRED_FPS_SDF_ENGINE_EXPANSION_PLAN.md`, `SDF_MANIFOLD_HIGH_FPS_ACCELERATION_PLAN.md`). Cutting the iteration budget or forcing a minimum step, which is what the August campaign did, buys frames by punching holes in thin geometry; that trade was already made once and reverted.




18. In Sanctum of Beginnings, two red shapes stacked on top of each other render very strangely, with corners that look like they're warping depending on my location relative to it.

    **One defect found and corrected 2026-08-31 — mechanism only, NOT confirmed to be this.** The exact-SDF branch of the WebGPU marcher implements Keinert et al. 2014 "Enhanced Sphere Tracing" §3.1 over-relaxation, and its rollback was wrong. On detecting an overstep it rolled `t` back to the safe position correctly and then **spent `d` from the sample taken at the overstepped point**. A signed distance field is 1-Lipschitz, which permits that value to be *larger* than the radius at the corrected position — so the recovery step could itself overshoot, and a ray could pass through the surface. It bites hardest where curvature is highest and the relaxation is most aggressive: a torus's inner ring, a rounded box's corners. And it is view-dependent, because whether a given ray oversteps at all depends on where it entered — which is what "warping depending on my location relative to it" describes. Fixed to discard the bad sample and re-evaluate at the corrected position, as the paper specifies.

    **What I could not do is prove this is Zach's artifact.** `webgpu_sdf_parity_test` compares SILHOUETTES, and a ray passing through the middle of a solid shape leaves the silhouette pixel-perfect — the test is structurally blind to it. I added interior-hole detection there and then re-injected the original rollback: it produced **zero** holes at 32x32, so the check did not witness the defect and is documented in-file as never having fired. Reaching this class needs a higher render resolution or a DEPTH comparison rather than a coverage one. **Zach: look at the two stacked red shapes again and say whether the corners still warp.** If they do, the cause is something else and this fix was still worth making on its own terms.

20. Loaded up chess_app. The chess pieces are not rendering except the 4 rooks, except when I am very close to them. They still seem to move and respond to clicks fine though.

    **Found, reproduced headlessly, fixed and locked (2026-08-31).** A march budget compared against the wrong origin. `misc.z` is a LENGTH — `maxDim * 8`, how far to march *inside* the bounding volume — and the marcher bounded itself with `maxDist = min(box.y, inst.misc.z)` while `t` is measured from the **eye**. The four rooks are the only pieces that are `ShapeKind::Cube` (kind 0), which draws through the **mesh** path and never touches the marcher; the pawns are Spheres, knights Ellipsoids, bishops Cones, queens Ovoids, kings Cylinders — all analytic, all raymarched, all gone.
