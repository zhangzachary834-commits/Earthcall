# The Second Person Framework

**What must be true before a second Person enters — Person-to-Person visibility,
representation, and conflict, specified at population one.**

**Status:** Specified only. Nothing below is implemented, and nothing below is needed
yet — *that is the point*: each of these three questions becomes a crisis if it is
first asked in production, and today they can be designed in peace. Commissioned by
the world's author 2026-08-19, in response to
`docs/Reflections on Trends and Directions/Reflections on Trajectory/The_Second_Person_and_the_Speed_of_Frameworks.md` §5.
Decisions marked **⚑ AUTHOR** are the world's author's to make, not an agent's —
the same rule as appending to a Singularity enum. An agent implementing this
framework stops at every flag and asks.

**Companion docs:** `NO_BLACK_BOX.md` (the substrate axis this deliberately does not
reopen), `docs/core/EarthcallOurverse.md` (Body/Voice doctrine; the representational-
subordination note), `OURVERSE.md` (due-weight metalaws, the unowned gathering Zone),
`LAW_AND_CREATION_SYSTEM.md` (authority, jurisdiction), `HIERARCHY_OF_JOYS.md`,
`AUTHORED_CATEGORIES.md`.

## 0. Why this exists now

Every hard guarantee in this tree — the infrasound floor, the authority clamp, the
sixth refusal's total legibility, the gathering Zone that refuses an owner — has been
exercised at **population one**: one Person, many First Movers. The manifesto's first
word is *Our*, and its central claims are claims about relation between Persons;
at population one they are structurally untestable. `Singularity/Network` will
eventually carry a second Person. Three frameworks must exist before that day.

The three are one family. Each is a question about what crosses the boundary
between two Persons — sight, likeness, or will — and each already has half its
machinery built for the substrate case.

| Axis | The question | Extends | Must NOT duplicate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visibility** (§1) | what may B's laws *read* of A? | `TransferPolicy`, `Relation` | a second permission system |
| **Representation** (§2) | what may B *show* of A? | kernel guards, `Relation`, Body doctrine | a rights-management subsystem |
| **Conflict** (§3) | whose law *fires* when equals contradict? | authority, jurisdiction, `ChangeRecorder` | a scheduler priority hack |

## 1. Axis one: read-visibility between Persons

"Privacy" conflates two questions, and refusal #6 already settled one of them:

- **Substrate legibility** — may the *law system* see a field? Settled: yes, totally
  (`NO_BLACK_BOX.md`). This framework does not reopen it.
- **Person-to-Person disclosure** — may *another Person's* law read this Person's
  state? Unsettled — and "unsettled" currently means **accidentally total**. Refusal
  #6 made every field reachable; nothing yet distinguishes who is reaching. That is
  the black-box error in mirror image: an ungoverned default, granted by accident,
  to whoever arrives second.

**The principle: disclosure rides Relations.** Perception of another Person is a
Relation, not a transfer (the manifesto's own resolution of the perceived-Voice
question). What B may read of A is a function of the Relation A has authored toward
B — a Relationship *is* the unit of disclosure. This is why no new machinery is
admitted: `Relation` already exists as a first-class being, and `TransferPolicy` is
already the one gate. The gate gains a **subject dimension** — today it answers
"may this path be written?"; it must learn to answer "may this path be read, *by a
law whose author stands in this Relation to the owner*?" One gate, one new
question, zero new systems.

- The gates themselves are registered properties (refusal #6 applies to the gate:
  A can see what A has disclosed and to whom; hiding the disclosure table would be
  a black box about black boxes).
- A **Kernel floor** exists: some minimum is universally visible — plausibly, that
  a Person exists and is present in a shared Zone; a co-present Person who cannot
  be perceived *at all* is a ghost, and ghosts are a consent violation in the other
  direction. **⚑ AUTHOR:** the exact contents of that floor.
- **⚑ AUTHOR:** the default posture between Persons with *no* authored Relation —
  open-by-default (the Ourverse instinct: trust first, gather freely) or
  closed-by-default (the dignity instinct: disclosure is a gift, not a leak). This
  is the single most consequential unmade decision in the framework, it is a
  theological decision, and no agent may make it.

## 2. Axis two: consent of representation

The manifesto already holds the doctrine; this axis is only its conversion into
mechanism. Body and Voice are non-instrumental; derivative Objects taking their data
are **subordinate visibilities of the Person, never lateral peers**; the Singular
Body, not the derived Object, is the source of truth; and correspondence to the
*real* human's body or voice exists only through that human's authored constraint.

As mechanism:

1. **Provenance is unbreakable.** An Object derived from a Person's Body/Voice
   carries a `derived-from` Relation at Kernel tier — read-only, never detachable
   by law, surviving serialization. Deleting the Relation *is* deleting the Object.
2. **Channels check at the boundary.** Any channel about to render, sound, export,
   or transmit a representation of a Person resolves the `derived-from` chain and
   the source Person's authored constraint *in C++, unconditionally* — the same
   pattern as the infrasound floor, because this too is a guard on the path to a
   Person (this time, to their likeness).
3. **Refusal is loud.** The channel names what it refused and whose constraint
   refused it — never a silent filter (`NO_BLACK_BOX.md` §2a: refuse out loud;
   never refuse by concealment).
4. **The real body is a higher order still.** Mapping any representation to the
   actual human (photos, FaceTime-shaped features, medical models) is gated by
   explicit authored constraint, never by default, and the manifesto's instruction
   stands: every guardrail on the symbolic Body is held "in many, many orders of
   greater and higher seriousness" here. **⚑ AUTHOR:** the shape of that authored
   constraint (what it names, how it is revoked) before the first such feature.

## 3. Axis three: horizontal law conflict

The authority hierarchy resolves *vertical* conflict — lower authority may not
govern higher, and the clamp on every file-read path enforces it. It says nothing
about two Persons of **equal** authority authoring contradictory laws over shared
ground. Materials already on hand: Zone jurisdiction (agenda item 11, unbuilt),
the Ourverse due-weight metalaws, `ChangeRecorder`, and the transparent-failure
doctrine.

The resolution ladder, in order — a law's write that another equal law contradicts
is resolved by the *first* rung that answers:

1. **Jurisdiction.** Whose Zone hosts the write? A Zone's owner's laws take
   precedence inside it — a Home is not a commons. The gathering Zone has no owner,
   deliberately, so this rung *cannot* answer there; that is by design, not a gap.
2. **Covenant.** An authored Relation *between the two laws' authors* (or the laws
   themselves — Relations between Laws are already admissible, since Laws are
   Singulars) that names precedence for a stated scope. Conflict resolution as an
   authored being, with provenance — not as an engine rule. This is the rung the
   manifesto's relational ontology wants to carry the weight.
3. **Loud refusal.** Absent both: **neither write lands**, and the conflict is
   published as an event naming both laws, both authors, and the contested path.
   Refusing both and saying so beats silently picking one — a Person can author a
   covenant in response to a named conflict, and can never respond to a silent one.

Never admissible as a resolution rung: timestamp, load order, id comparison, or
any other accident of the machine. **Accidents must not govern Persons.**
Resolution is bounded like everything else (`kMaxChainRounds` doctrine): a
covenant pair whose laws re-trigger each other is the cascade loop of
`INTERACTION_AS_LAW.md` §6b, and it is detected, not survived.
**⚑ AUTHOR:** whether rung 2 requires *mutual* authorship (both Persons sign the
covenant) — the marriage shape — or accepts unilateral deference.

## 4. The two scopes: the agent society is the wind tunnel

This framework has a rehearsal instance running today. The development process is
already population-many — multiple agents with overlapping jurisdiction, a
stakes-classification rule (intercom rule 4), threads visible to all, approval
gates for irreversible acts, conflicting edits to shared ground. Agents are not
Persons (refusal #5 stands, always), but the *governance shapes* are the same, one
level up — the same way `docs/architecture/Integration/` and
`src/Singularity/Foreign/` are one effort at two scopes.

| Agent-scope instance | Framework axis | Precedent it already produced |
|---|---|---|
| intercom rule 4, admission test (agenda: Housekeeping 8) | conflict / standing | "high stakes" self-classified is no gate at all |
| `AGENTS.md` "update this document" drift | covenant vs mechanism | telling is not a mechanism; compile instructions into checks |
| sandbox-to-terminal bridge (default-deny, typed approval) | kernel guard on reach | a working guard, probed against five evasions |
| all-threads-readable intercom | visibility default | open-by-default worked *for agents*; that is evidence, not the decision (**⚑ AUTHOR** still owns §1's default for Persons) |

Every governance failure observed in the agent society is a test case for this
framework. Harvest them: when an intercom conflict, a stale covenant, or a
visibility surprise occurs, record it here as a precedent the way the corpus
records shipped bugs.

## 5. Admission test — before shipping ANY multi-Person feature

Four questions, in order. Stop at the first **NO**.

```
Q1  Does every read of one Person's state by another Person's law pass the one
    gate, carrying the Relation between them?
        NO → you have built around TransferPolicy or invented a second gate. STOP.

Q2  Does every representation of a Person that crosses a channel resolve its
    derived-from chain and the source Person's constraint, refusing OUT LOUD?
        NO → the channel is a deepfake path with better manners. STOP.

Q3  Is every equal-authority conflict on the feature's paths resolved by
    jurisdiction, covenant, or loud mutual refusal — and by no accident of the
    machine?
        NO → an accident is governing Persons. STOP.

Q4  Are all ⚑ AUTHOR decisions the feature touches actually decided — by the
    author, recorded in this document?
        NO → the feature is ahead of its constitution. STOP.
```

## 6. What this framework refuses

| Refused | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| a `PrivacyManager` / second permission system | one was built beside `TransferPolicy` and deleted; two gates that can disagree are the absence of governance | the one gate, with a subject dimension |
| a `Friend` / `Contact` / `Permission` class | refusal #1 — a friendship is a `Relation`, authored, with provenance | `Relation` / `Relationship` |
| visibility flags as unregistered fields | refusal #6 applies to the gates themselves | disclosure state is registered, legible to its owner |
| perceiver-owned copies of another's Body/Voice data | manifesto: representational subordination; ownership never transfers by perception | `derived-from` at Kernel tier; the Body is source of truth |
| resolving conflicts by timestamp / load order / id | accidents must not govern Persons | the ladder in §3 |
| deciding §1's stranger-default in C++ silently | it is a theological decision | **⚑ AUTHOR** |
