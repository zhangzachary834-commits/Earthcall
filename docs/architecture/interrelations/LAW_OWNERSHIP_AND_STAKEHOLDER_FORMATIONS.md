## The Interrelation

In conventional software, a capability or script belongs entirely to its author or the system administrator. If other users come to depend on that script (e.g., a shared tool within a company), they are vulnerable to "bereavement"—the author can retire, delete, or modify the script unilaterally, causing cascading failures for the dependents. This is a symptom of an asymmetrical, enclosed ownership model.

Earthcall resolves this by unifying **Law** and **Ownership** through **Relation Formations**.

Because Laws are authored beings that modify explicit properties, any Object or Zone that a Law affects becomes fundamentally intertwined with that Law. When multiple Persons bring their Objects into the scope of a Law (e.g., relying on a shared workflow Law within an organizational Zone), they do not merely "use" the Law; their dependence is modeled as a structural Relation.

### Ownership as a Distributed Graph
In Earthcall, ownership is not a binary access control list. It is an emergent property of the Relation Formation.
When a Law's changes are constitutive (load-bearing, scarce, or structurally critical) to the affected Objects, the Persons owning those Objects automatically become stakeholders in the Law's ownership Formation. They join the shared ownership (conceptually similar to a distributed `shared_ptr`) with agreed-upon roles.

This prevents sudden bereavement. Retiring or significantly altering a widely-depended-upon Law requires navigating the stakeholder Formation. Because the Law's effects are explicitly known via the Prophetic Rete, the system can precisely track which Objects—and therefore which Persons—are structurally impacted by a proposed change, ensuring that unilateral destruction of shared value is structurally impossible.
