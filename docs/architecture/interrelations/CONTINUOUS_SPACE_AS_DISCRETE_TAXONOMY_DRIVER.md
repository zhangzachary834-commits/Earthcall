## The Interrelation

In many simulation environments, space is treated merely as a coordinate grid where discrete objects happen to reside. An object's category (what it *is*) is fundamentally detached from its position in continuous space.

Earthcall allows space itself to actively dictate taxonomy. This is achieved by combining the exact continuous spatial bounds of `ONTOMATH_FRAMEWORK.md` with the dynamic, authored DAG categories defined in `AUTHORED_CATEGORIES.md`.

In Earthcall, a spatial region is not defined by an invisible trigger box, but by a continuous Signed Distance Field (SDF) or Scalar Field (OntoMath). Because these fields define exact mathematical boundaries, the `PHYSICS_AND_COLLISION.md` system can deterministically track when an object's bounds intersect the field.

This continuous spatial intersection fires a discrete `PhysicsCollisionEvent` to the Event Bus.

Because taxonomy is not a hardcoded C++ type but a dynamic graph of `Relation`s (`AUTHORED_CATEGORIES.md`), a Person can author a Law that listens for this specific collision event.

When the discrete event (generated by continuous movement) reaches the Law:
1. The Law evaluates the properties of the Object entering the field.
2. The Law mutates the semantic graph, adding or removing `Relation` edges that define the Object's taxonomy.

For example, a Person can define a mathematical field representing a "Purification Zone." When an Object (e.g., a "Cursed Sword") continuously moves until its mathematical bounds intersect the field, the resulting event triggers a Law that immediately severs the "Cursed" taxonomic relation and mints a "Purified" relation.

**Conclusion:** The exactness of OntoMath combined with the fluidity of Authored Categories allows spatial movement to act as a compiler for meaning. Space is not passive; intersecting a continuous mathematical boundary is sufficient to structurally redefine the discrete nature of a being.
