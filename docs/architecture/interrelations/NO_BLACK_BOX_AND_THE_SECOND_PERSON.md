# No Black Box and the Second Person Framework

**Legibility of state does not confer authority to read, write, represent, or move another Person. The intended boundary is not yet a universal runtime gate.**

## Interrelated Sources

- [No Black Box](../ontology/NO_BLACK_BOX.md): meaningful being state must be registered and addressable, or explicitly identified as machine mechanism beneath the Kernel; readable does **not** mean universally writable.
- [The Second Person Framework](../ourverse/SECOND_PERSON_FRAMEWORK.md): specifies distinct Person-to-Person questions of visibility, representation, and horizontal conflict, and identifies what is still unbuilt.

## The architectural distinction

The sixth refusal forbids a hidden state lever that no authored Law can inspect or govern. It does **not** require every registered Property to be mutable: computed/read-only Properties remain legible without accepting writes. Nor does a qualified `PropertyPath` establish who is asking, why they have standing, or whether an operation should be admitted.

The Second Person Framework adds questions of **whose state**, **which operation**, **which actor**, and **which Relation or constitutional ground authorizes it**. Authorship, ownership, governance, dependency, and Constitution must not be collapsed into a single hardcoded permission flag. Visibility, representation, and conflicts between Persons are distinct axes; proposed decisions marked **AUTHOR** remain decisions for Earthcall's human author.

## Implementation boundary: existing versus proposed

1. **Existing set-to-set gate:** `TransferPolicy::canTransfer` governs set-to-set transfer. It is the existing authority office to extend, not evidence that ordinary `PropertyPath` reads/writes already enforce Person-scoped consent.
2. **Not yet implemented generally:** The canonical No Black Box specification explicitly says ordinary `PropertyPath` reads and writes do **not** pass through a general access decision. `PropertyPath::setValue` alone has no actor context. Do not describe a universal signed-consent interception as shipped.
3. **Limited intrinsic guards:** Certain positive Person body/location actuation channels have Kernel refusals, including documented Law motion and foreign WebSocket paths. These do not prove complete channel coverage; the in-process actuation audit remains open.
4. **Specified future Person boundaries:** The Second Person Framework calls for Relation-borne disclosure standing, signed/revocable consent for relevant bodily actuation, likeness provenance and representation guards, and conflict adjudication. Its three Second-Person mechanisms are **specifications**, not implemented end-to-end guarantees. The Primary Home admission invariant is separately implemented.

The intended synthesis is **legibility without coercion**: expose the state and the applicable policy as addressable truths, while checking standing at each relevant read, write, transfer, representation, or actuation boundary. That requires explicit actor context, lawful decisions, and loud refusals where a channel is constitutionally guarded—not concealment, not a second competing permission subsystem, and not a claim that the missing gates already exist.
