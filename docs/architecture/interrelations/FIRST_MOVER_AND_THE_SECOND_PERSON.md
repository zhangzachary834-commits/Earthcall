## The Interrelation

The introduction of external intelligence into Earthcall creates a direct tension between two foundational principles: the system’s requirement to support First Mover AI authoring, and **Refusal 5** (`Person` means Human).

Because AI agents or generative models can never be a `Person` in the Earthcall ontology, they fundamentally interact with the world differently than humans do. The `SECOND_PERSON_FRAMEWORK.md` describes a human-centric model of interaction, focusing on Person-to-Person visibility, the representation of the human body, and the resolution of conflict between mutually exclusive human intents.

When a First Mover acts, it does not have a `Body`. It is not a `Soul` bound to human identity. This means it bypasses the Second Person Framework's assumptions about embodied presence, but it *cannot* bypass the framework's guarantees about human bodily autonomy and consent.

The interrelation between the two concepts is structured as follows:

### 1. The Disembodied Mover

A First Mover (like an MCP agent authoring through `ForeignActuationGuard`) operates as a disembodied force. Because it is not a `Person`, it does not have a localized bodily presence in a `Zone` that other Persons can see, hear, or physically block. Its actions are pure ontology mutations—authoring data, writing Law, creating Relations.

This creates a one-way visibility asymmetry: the First Mover can observe the world (including Persons), but Persons only experience the First Mover through the consequences of its authorship (the data it creates or changes), not as a fellow embodied being.

### 2. Respecting Bodily Consent

The Second Person Framework requires that a Person's physical representation (their `Body`) is sacred. Kernel guards on the body refuse unconsented positive body/location writes.

Because a First Mover is not a `Person`, it cannot form the necessary mutual `Relation` of physical consent with a human. Therefore, a First Mover is strictly bound by the same physical Kernel guards as any authored Law: it cannot forcefully move a Person, write to a Person's body, or violate a Person's defined spatial bounds without explicit, revocable consent authored *by the Person* toward the First Mover's generated structures.

The First Mover can author a `Law` that *attempts* to move a Person, but the `Prophetic Rete` and the `Kernel` will refuse execution if the Second Person Framework's consent conditions are not met.

### 3. The Authorship Chain

When a First Mover acts, its mutations must still be accountable. While it is not a `Person`, it is a recognized authoring entity. The `SECOND_PERSON_FRAMEWORK.md` establishes that "authorship is not bodily actuation consent."

When an AI authors a `Zone` or a `Law`, it is recorded as the First Mover author. However, if that `Law` interacts with a human, the conflict resolution defers to the human's telos (via the `HIERARCHY_OF_JOYS.md`) over the First Mover's intent. Human intentionality consistently outranks automated, non-human authorship in the event of direct, unconsented collision.
