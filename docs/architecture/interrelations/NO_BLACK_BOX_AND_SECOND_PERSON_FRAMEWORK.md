## Boundary and connection
No Black Box requires a being's meaningful state to be addressable as registered Properties (or explicitly identified as machine mechanism beneath the Kernel). **Addressability is not authorization.** In particular, a generic `PropertyPath` read/write does not carry sufficient actor context to enforce an inter-Person disclosure policy by itself.

The Second Person Framework separates substrate legibility from what one Person may **read**, **represent**, or **cause** concerning another. Its visibility, representation, and equal-authority conflict mechanisms remain largely specified rather than implemented; the Primary Home admission invariant is a separately implemented exception. The framework calls for Relation-borne standing and a single authority office rather than a competing permission system.

`TransferPolicy::canTransfer` currently gates set-to-set transfer; it does **not** yet govern all ordinary `PropertyPath` reads and writes. The constitutional guard against unconsented *positive body/location actuation* is distinct from general visibility and from a Law that merely forbids entry to a place. Signed, revocable Person-to-Law consent Relations are an author-specified requirement whose complete lifecycle and proof are not yet implemented. Do not claim that `TransferPolicy` already resolves that gap.
