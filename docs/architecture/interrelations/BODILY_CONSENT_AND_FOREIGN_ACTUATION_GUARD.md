## The Interrelation

Earthcall establishes a severe constraint on the movement and manipulation of the human form: **Kernel guards on the body are not settings.** A moving Law needs the Person's signed, revocable consent sub-Relation; authorship alone is insufficient. This is the **Bodily Consent** rule.

Simultaneously, Earthcall enforces strict limits on foreign software and models via the **Foreign Actuation Guard**. Modality channels (such as MCP or foreign Python processes) operate under `Singularity/Foreign/` and must pass the `ForeignActuationGuard` as a Person-granted First Mover.

The interrelation between these two aspects forms a unified defense of the Person's sovereignty. Because foreign processes act as First Movers, they are bound by the exact same ontological constraints as in-world Laws. A foreign model cannot send a network packet that simply teleports a Person, nor can it bypass the `Singularity/TransferPolicy` to write directly to a body's properties.

When a foreign process attempts to enact a change upon the world, it must resolve its intent through the exact same channels that an authored Law uses. If that intent involves the body of a Person, it immediately hits the kernel-level Bodily Consent guard. The foreign model must acquire a signed, revocable consent sub-Relation from the Person, just like any other entity.

This ensures that the "No Black Box" and "Person means Human" rules are upheld not just internally, but universally across any integration. The Foreign Actuation Guard does not need a separate, parallel permission system for bodily integrity; it simply relies on the bedrock of Bodily Consent. The external world is mapped into the internal ontology, and the internal ontology's ultimate boundary is the consent of the human Person.
