Earthcall's explicit separation of `Timeline` (a temporal domain), `Moment` (an instant/interval), and `Event` (a distinguished Moment with participants) has profound implications when a Second Person enters the system.

Because `Timeline` inherits from `Singular`, it is an authored, first-class being, not an ambient, global C++ constant (like `float dt`). This means different Singulars can be bound to different Timelines.

The Second Person Framework introduces the reality of Person A and Person B coexisting. But what if Person A is bound to Timeline Alpha (perhaps a local simulation, a temporal pocket, or a paused state), while Person B is bound to Timeline Beta (the universal baseline)?

When Person A attempts to interact with Person B, we face a clash not just of spatial authority, but of temporal substrate.

The Second Person Framework governs *conflict* (whose law fires when equals contradict?). But before a conflict can even be evaluated, the `Event` must be situated in Time. If Person A strikes Person B, when did it happen?

### Thoughts on Integration and Interrelation

The intersection requires that the `TransferPolicy` (the gatekeeper of interactions) must mediate across Timelines.

If Person A (on Timeline Alpha) writes to a property on Person B (on Timeline Beta), the resulting `Event` must reconcile the two temporal domains. Does the Event belong to Alpha, Beta, or a parent Timeline that encompasses both?

The Hierarchy of Joys likely dictates the resolution. The temporal domain of the "higher" joy or the more fundamental Relation might absorb the Event. For instance, if Person B owns their body (a Constitutional Relation), their native Timeline (Beta) might dictate the authoritative `Moment` when the physical strike is recorded, even if Person A initiated the action from a divergent temporal domain (Alpha).

Furthermore, the visibility aspect of the Second Person Framework (what may B read of A?) now includes a temporal dimension. B might have permission to read A's spatial state, but if A is on a different Timeline, B is reading A's state *at a specific Moment relative to B's Timeline*. The mapping between Timeline Alpha and Timeline Beta becomes a critical property that the Prophetic Rete must evaluate when predicting Law execution. The Second Person Framework ensures that temporal relativity doesn't become a black box—the relationship between the two Timelines must be explicitly authored and legible to the system.
