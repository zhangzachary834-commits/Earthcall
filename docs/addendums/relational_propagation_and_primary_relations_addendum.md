## 1. Systemic Propagation and Closed vs Open Systems

Earthcall demands that changes do not happen in isolated silos. When a subsystem changes, its effects must propagate—what the architecture calls **Relational propagation**.

If a relation changes, it ripples to other relations and other Singulars. The architecture requires that any change that interlocks another system, or propagates beyond its own immediate recursion, must be **known**. It cannot be a silent update. The system must be capable of recognizing these cycles and ripples.

## 2. Primary Relations as the Proposed Enduring Record

`PRIMARY_AND_SUB_RELATIONS.md` proposes a future distinction between Primary Relations and Sub-Relations for preserving relational history without making that history an event-replay log. That architecture is explicitly **not yet built**; current engine paths can still erase Relations outright.

When a premise changes—for example, if a Law was conditioned on an object being "red" but it turns "blue"—the sub-Relation built on that premise becomes logically impossible.

Under that proposed architecture, instead of erasing the connection between the involved Singulars entirely:
-   **Sub-Relations dissolve or change kind:** The active variant is removed from the active set because it is no longer true now. It changes to a historical kind.
-   **Primary Relations endure:** The foundational "Primary" kind relation between the two Singulars remains. It keeps a record of existence and a mathematical model of its past.

## 3. Integration Thoughts: Propagation Leaves a Trace

The integration suggests a prospective architecture: **Primary Relations could provide an enduring relational-history substrate for effects of Relational propagation, without themselves becoming an event log.**

If this architecture is implemented, when a cyclical change or systemic ripple invalidates relational state, affected active sub-Relations can re-kind into historical variants while remaining connected to their Primary Relation.

That would preserve a record that the relevant sub-Relation existed together with the proposed OntoMath model of its past. It does **not**, by itself, establish that every propagation occurrence is permanently logged or that the Systemic Maxim's requirement that propagation "must be known" is already fulfilled. The precise mechanism that makes propagation legible to the system remains future architecture.
