## Primitives as Brushstrokes, Not Bound Identities

The "Maple Wood Kids Building & Stacking Blocks failure mode" warns against confusing the available primitive vocabulary (boxes, cylinders, spheres) with the architectural identity of the finished form. A box is not a wall; it is merely a primitive participating in the construction of one.

This design doctrine perfectly aligns with the ontological stance on Pixel Substrate Process and Authored Identity. Just as a rendered region of pixels can act simultaneously as raw machine substrate, the result of a process, and a vessel of authored identity, the geometric primitives used to build a 3D structure are fluid.

1.  **Substrate:** The primitives are the mathematical substrate (the SDFs, the parameters) that the engine processes.
2.  **Process:** The composition of these primitives—blending, carving, Boolean operations—is the authored act that produces the manifestation of a wall or a column.
3.  **Authored Identity:** The resulting unified structure (the wall, the temple) is the Singular entity that carries the authored identity, not the individual constituent boxes and cylinders.

By embracing this, we avoid treating every mathematical operation or primitive shape as a distinct Being. The architecture remains cohesive, and the visual form becomes a true representation of the authored intent, free from the rigid seams of "toy blocks." The identity resides in the whole, even as the substrate is composed of many parts.
