## The primordial event

The surviving July record says that on **2026-07-06**, the design direction settled around one architectural pivot:

> Law conditions and actions should be plain-data expression trees over PropertyPaths, not opaque `std::function` closures.

That is the egg.

Closures could execute, but they could not honestly become authored world-stuff: they did not serialize, could not be inspected, could not be synthesized, and could not be edited as meaning. So the early system took the same move geometry had taken with `SdfNode`: store structure as data, then compile/evaluate it.

From that pivot came, in absurdly rapid succession:

- `ConditionModel` and `ActionModel`
- PropertyPath-addressable state
- Event → Rete → Apply
- ChangeRecorder
- ObjectConcept / set-to-set creation
- the Law Author
- Law synthesis
- Metalaws
- authority ceilings
- continuous Laws
- OntoMath bindings
- authored perception
- persistence by stable identity
- Zones becoming beings with ownership and Home

By **2026-07-09**, the recorded build order already read like the genome of modern Earthcall: property bridge → models → hearing loop → recorder → concept/spawn → graph UI → synthesis + Metalaws.

The old note even says the important part out loud: once conditions and actions are trees of data over addressable properties, later features become *operations on trees* instead of repeated re-architecture.

So, yes: a shocking amount of Robot City is urban sprawl over a July campfire.

## The ancient authority fossil

This is the funniest part because it connects directly to what Sol just picked from the current To-do list.

The July system deliberately made Laws legible enough that a Law targeting another Law *is just a Metalaw*. But one thing was kept outside the legible property surface:

`authorityLevel`.

The ancient reasoning was understandable: authority must be **granted, never claimed**, therefore do not make it law-writable.

The surviving July directive later summarized the rule as:

> wrap almost ALL major members/pointers into properties — full legibility as the default; the only exceptions are things deliberately kept source-code-only (like authorityLevel).

That exception survived the primordial era.

Then September Earthcall developed a stricter No-Black-Box doctrine and realized the deeper distinction:

**visibility is not mutability.**

Hiding authority does not secure it; it merely makes governance unable to inspect governance.

So Mythos returned on 2026-09-24, excavated the old seam, and pointed at the mature solution already present elsewhere in the substrate:

make `authority` and `authors` **legible but Kernel-tier read-only**.

Not a new permission system. Not a reversal of the original anti-tyranny intent. The same intent, finally expressed in the stronger ontology.

Which means the current task is basically Earthcall walking back into its own Cambrian layer and saying:

> "The instinct was right. The mechanism can now grow up."
