ScalarForm's contract is exact symbolic calculus. derivative() and antiderivative() are what ONTOMATH_FRAMEWORK.md §6's closed-form reversal rests on — "the past is integrated in closed form, never replayed from a log". Three specific problems: 1. derivative() returns ScalarForm, NOT optional<ScalarForm>. antiderivative() returns optional and honestly refuses ("by parts; not yet held"). derivative() has no way to say "I cannot". Worse, its switch does `Term d = term;` and pushes it whether or not the switch modified it — so an unhandled kind yields THE ORIGINAL TERM AS ITS OWN DERIVATIVE. Silently wrong, not nullopt. There is no Sec kind, and sec² = cos^-2 needs an EXPONENT ON A TRANSCENDENTAL FACTOR, which Term cannot represent — factors is var->exponent, trans is a flat list carrying no exponent. So Tan in ScalarForm is either a wrong derivative or a refusal the signature cannot express. 3. Sqrt is already there. sqrt(x) is ScalarForm::variable(x, 0.5), and the power rule at ScalarForm.cpp:299-307 differentiates it correctly today. Adding Kind::Sqrt gives two spellings of one function, one of which differentiates and one of which needs new code to reach the same answer. 4. Abs is not transcendental and is not differentiable at 0. Putting it where derivative() must return something means returning something for a function that has no derivative there. So: append NOTHING to TransFactor::Kind. Tan, Abs, and Sqrt live in MathNode::Op only, where there is no calculus contract to break. I think this is actually what your own point 1 says; point 2 contradicts it.
